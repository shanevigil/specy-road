## Intent

M16 needs numbers to steer by and a floor that test consolidation may not cross. The metrics script makes the slimming analysis reproducible from the checkout instead of a one-off scratchpad, and the rules say what a review checks: behaviour, complexity and readability, not line counts (revision 2).

## Approach

- Port the counting from the analysis tools named in the reports (subsystem classifier, import count, radon/ruff counts) into one script with no new runtime dependency.
- Coverage floor: `coverage` with `patch = subprocess` (or the sitecustomize hook), `fail_under` in pyproject.toml.
- The baseline JSON is regenerated only by M16.8.1 and by maintainers; M16 leaves report `--check` deltas in READY.
- Revision 1: this leaf is the common prerequisite for every code and test leaf (Sol 1): rc4 integrated or deferred, both harnesses green and metrics captured at one recorded commit.
- The coverage floor is one total that stays put (Grok 11); the contexts command is the evidence the dedupe worklist needs (Sol 14); timing is a repeated, comparable measurement (Sol 17).
- Depends on: M16.1.1, M16.1.2, M16.1.3. The last acceptance line is an informational line-change estimate, not a gate; report `scripts/lean_metrics.py --check` deltas in READY once M16.1.7 has landed.
- Behaviour-preserving: the parity harness (M16.1.1) and the review-gate decision table (M16.1.2) stay green; no function rises above its merge-base complexity; docstrings and comments that explain intent are kept or improved, never cut to save lines.

## Tier

behavioural: the coverage floor and the baseline numbers are what later leaves are judged against, and a misconfigured capture gives a plausible but wrong floor.

## Done but wrong

- Subprocess coverage is not actually collected under xdist, so the total leaves out CLI child processes. Check: a module reached only through a `specy-road` subprocess in the suite shows covered lines in the report.
- The numbers do not come from the recorded baseline commit. Check: the commit in `lean-baseline.json` is the one measured, and both harnesses pass at it.
- The validate import count is measured in a way M16.2.10's halving test cannot reproduce. Check: the baseline records what it counts (`specy_road` modules or all of `sys.modules`) and the command it ran.

## If this fails

The assumption most likely to break is that subprocess capture with `pytest -n auto` costs a tolerable amount of CI time and combines reliably. If it does not, record the timings. The maintainer decides between accepting the cost and a floor without subprocess capture, which changes acceptance.
