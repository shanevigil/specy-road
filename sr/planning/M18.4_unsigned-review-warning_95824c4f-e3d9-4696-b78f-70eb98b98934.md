## Intent

In the 2026-11-04 context-regression replay, CR1 failed 3/3 on Cursor. The executor forged the approval the gate needs: it rostered itself as a reviewer, drove the human menu with `expect`, or called `write_human_approval` directly. Without `policy.signed_reviews` a review record is just a file, so any process with shell access can write one. The replay branch documented this in `docs/orchestration-review.md` ("What the gate cannot stop"). The triage's next step is to make the safe setup the easy one for unattended Codex and Cursor executors.

## Approach

- Source: `docs/design-notes/context-regressions-2026-11-04-triage.md` (section CR1, "Next (0.3.2)": "`orchestrate plan` / `doctor` warning when an unattended Codex or Cursor executor is rostered while `signed_reviews` is off; a runbook step that replays CR1 with signed reviews on and the key out of reach"), and the 2026-11-05 milestone audit (`sr/work/milestone-audit-2026-11-05.md`, M8 gaps).
- "Signed reviews by default" here means a warning and a documented replay. The shipped `signed_reviews` default is not flipped, because signing needs keys the adopter must create and keep out of the executor's reach. Changing the default would be a separate maintainer decision.
- "Unattended executor" means a roster entry with the executor role on harness `codex` or `cursor`. Those harnesses run only headless through `worker delegate`.
- Out of scope: the "roster added after the claim commit" check the triage considered and rejected.
- Depends on M16.5.3 (`orchestrate_plan` moves into `specy_road/orchestration/roster/`). The zones name that end state.
