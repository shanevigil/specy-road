## Tier

behavioural: one function replaces six eligibility checks that decide launches and the gate; the corpus can match while refusal order or a caller's defaults differ on rows it lacks.

## Done but wrong

- The first refusal is not pinned: the corpus has rows where two or more checks fail at once (for example paused and sensitivity), so each caller's first refusal is recorded.
- A caller keeps its own copy: a temporary change to one rule inside `check_assignment` changes that rule's corpus rows for all six callers.
- The corpus came from the new code: it is committed before the switch and passes on the merge base.

## If this fails

Most likely to break: the six callers agree at pickup. The corpus may show a divergence, the risk this leaf's Intent names. Acceptance keeps each caller's result, so the divergence goes into `context`, not a fix; the maintainer decides any fix, as its own leaf.
