## Intent

The operator owns the rules and must be able to decide a leaf's review outcome directly, without turning that decision into reviewer paperwork. On 2026-11-08 and 09 the maintainer accepted M16.1.6 as-is, and the tool still refused: a human approval only fills a missing reviewer family, and `review defer` needs an RCA record and refuses correctness findings. Source: maintainer analysis (private), 2026-11-09, section 4.

## Approach

The maintainer decided on 2026-11-09 that an operator decision is given in plain words, for example "approved" or "accept as-is" in the coordinator's chat, and the coordinator records it, with no terminal command. Reuse `review defer`'s decision-entry and marker machinery (`review_deferral.py`, read only), without its RCA requirement and refusal rules. The decision entry is the authority, and the marker is a cache. Risk accepted under the best-effort stance: an agent writes the record. It is contained by the role guard (coordinator only) and by the quote and session link the record carries, so the operator can audit or revoke it.

## Tasks / checklist

- [ ] `review operator-decision` command, coordinator role only
- [ ] Gate and status read the override
- [ ] Tests, docs, CHANGELOG

## Tier

trust boundary: review records and the gate.

## Done but wrong

- An executor or reviewer can record an operator decision: a launch in those roles is refused, and a hand-written marker without its decision entry has no effect.
- An override outlives its commit: after a new commit, the gate counts reviews again.

## If this fails

If the gate cannot read the override without widening into other gate modules, stop and name them; the maintainer decides.

## Accepted limits

- One leaf and one commit per record; the authority is the operator's quote or a standing rule; never a trust leaf.
